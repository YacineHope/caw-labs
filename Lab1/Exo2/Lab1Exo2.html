The Collapse of Care

The healthcare system in Gaza is on the brink of collapse. Since October 7th, 120 health facilities have been damaged, including 38 hospitals and 96 clinics, while 147 ambulances and 55 civil defense vehicles have been destroyed. With no fuel to run ambulances, hospitals struggling without electricity, and medical supplies running out, doctors are forced to treat patients in dire conditions. This has left thousands of wounded without proper care, turning hospitals into overcrowded shelters rather than functioning medical centers, and making even basic treatment nearly impossible.

The Silencing of Schools

The education system in Gaza is collapsing under the weight of war and destruction. Since October 7th, 156 schools and universities have been completely destroyed, and 382 more partially damaged, leaving hundreds of thousands of students without safe classrooms. With infrastructure ruined, teachers displaced, and resources scarce, learning has been forced to stop or move into overcrowded shelters. What was once a vibrant system nurturing future generations is now struggling to survive amid devastation.

Infrastructure Devastation

The destruction of Gaza’s infrastructure is staggering. Since October 7th, 210,000 housing units have been destroyed or severely damaged, with another 180,000 partially damaged, leaving 110,000 buildings completely uninhabitable. This scale of devastation has displaced hundreds of thousands of families, forcing many to live in overcrowded shelters or makeshift tents. Beyond homes, the collapse of infrastructure has crippled daily life—water, electricity, and sanitation systems are barely functioning, and entire neighborhoods have been reduced to rubble. These statistics reflect not only the physical loss of buildings but also the erasure of communities and the deep humanitarian crisis facing Gaza’s population.
